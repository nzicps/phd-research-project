## Summary of the scholarly context (as stated in the proposal)

- Traditional economic indicators alone are increasingly seen as
  insufficient for capturing wellbeing; there is a shift toward
  multidimensional approaches spanning economic, social, and
  health-related factors (Stiglitz et al., 2009; OECD, 2020).
- Health is a core component of human capital and a key determinant of
  economic participation, labour-market outcomes, productivity, and
  long-term economic stability (Conti & Heckman, 2010; Currie & Madrian,
  1999). Chronic conditions add constraints: reduced work capacity,
  increased healthcare needs, fluctuating capacity to participate.
- Entrepreneurship research has shifted from a pure growth/innovation
  lens toward its relationship with individual wellbeing — it can both
  enhance and constrain wellbeing depending on individual and contextual
  factors (Wiklund et al., 2019), and is closely linked to personal
  health, particularly under uncertainty and stress (Shepherd & Patzelt,
  2015).
- Institutional and policy environments shape both entrepreneurial
  performance and wellbeing (Stephan, Uhlaner & Stride, 2015).
- In Aotearoa New Zealand, the Treasury's Living Standards Framework
  provides a multidimensional wellbeing approach incorporating health,
  human capital, and economic participation as interconnected domains
  (NZ Treasury, 2021, 2022).
- **The gap:** health and entrepreneurship are typically studied and
  evaluated separately, limiting understanding of how investments across
  the two domains interact for individuals, particularly those with
  chronic health conditions. This thesis integrates the two within a
  unified analytical framework.
