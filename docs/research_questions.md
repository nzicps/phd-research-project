# Research Questions

## Provisional thesis title

**Developing an Integrated Framework for Evaluating Health and Entrepreneurship
Outcomes for Individuals with Chronic Health Conditions in Aotearoa New Zealand**

Applicant: Osman Hassan Osman · Department: Social Sciences ·
Proposed supervisor: Professor Barry Milne, University of Auckland.

## Overarching question

How do health and entrepreneurship investments interact to influence outcomes —
economic participation, income, stability, and wellbeing — for individuals living
with chronic health conditions in Aotearoa New Zealand?

## Aims (from the Statement of Research Intent)

1. Examine how chronic health conditions influence individuals' capacity to
   participate in economic activities, including employment and
   entrepreneurship (health-related factors: access to services, health
   status, functional capacity).
2. Examine how individuals with different health profiles engage in
   entrepreneurial activity, and how this engagement relates to income,
   economic stability, and wellbeing.
3. Analyse how health and entrepreneurship are currently conceptualised,
   measured, and supported within existing New Zealand policy and
   programme frameworks (via document analysis).
4. Develop an integrated evaluation framework that enables analysis of
   outcomes across health, economic participation, and wellbeing —
   supporting more holistic evaluation of public investment across both
   domains, which are currently designed and evaluated independently.

## Motivating gap

Health outcomes are typically assessed through clinical/wellbeing indicators
and entrepreneurship outcomes through economic/business-performance metrics,
evaluated independently of one another. This separation limits understanding
of how investments across the two domains interact at the individual level —
particularly for people with chronic health conditions, for whom
entrepreneurship may be both an alternative pathway to economic participation
*and* a source of additional demands, uncertainty, and pressure that could
affect health.

## Note on scope

This is a **mixed-methods** thesis: a quantitative component using linked
IDI/LBD administrative data (propensity-score matching, longitudinal
analysis), and a qualitative component (document analysis of NZ health and
entrepreneurship-support policy/programme materials) feeding into a single
integrated conceptual/evaluation framework. This repository currently
scaffolds the quantitative component only — see `docs/methodology.md` for
where the qualitative document-analysis component would sit.
